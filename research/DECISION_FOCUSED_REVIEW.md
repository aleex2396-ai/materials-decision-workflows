# Decision-focused moment learning for Wasserstein regret control

Research date: 2026-11-05. This is a focused prior-work check for the question: **does tuning a second-moment shrinkage estimator for transferred causal-control regret improve decisions over tuning the same estimator for likelihood, and which directions of estimation error matter?** It is not a comprehensive novelty search. Public papers, public GitHub resources and OpenAlex metadata were used; no private observations informed these queries.

## Position relative to existing work

Learning a probabilistic model for its downstream optimization loss is established. Differentiating convex control problems, learning covariance matrices for portfolio decisions, and learning optimal-transport ambiguity sets are also established. The defensible contribution here is a reproducible specialization and diagnostic study: a second-moment shrinkage learner coupled to the causal type-2 Wasserstein minimax-regret controller of Al Taha, Yan and Bitar; an independently checked implicit hypergradient; a precise distinction between optimized-value sensitivity and transferred-policy loss; and an explicit policy-ordering counterexample plus a spectral-margin transfer bound.

Whether the *particular combination* is absent from prior publications is unresolved. A bounded keyword search cannot establish novelty, and the broad ideas are plainly present in the sources below. Any empirical improvement must be reported with its finite-data conditions, failure cases and matched baselines. The derivations in [DECISION_FOCUSED_THEORY.md](../docs/DECISION_FOCUSED_THEORY.md) are reference-problem propositions rather than claims of new general theory.

## What is actually learned

The current experiment estimates the uncentered second moment of a declared zero-mean synthetic disturbance trajectory. The model family is diagonal shrinkage plus a trace-scaled positive ridge. It learns two parameters by differentiating a held-out validation loss through the causal controller solve. The likelihood baseline has the same two parameters, samples, bounds and iteration budget. Test outcomes enter neither fit.

The nominal law is explicitly full-dimensional Gaussian with the learned positive definite second moment; it is not an empirical discrete distribution silently substituted into an absolute-continuity theorem. The ambiguity radius is a declared study parameter. Training the nominal center for decisions does not prove that the resulting Wasserstein ball contains the data-generating law with any prescribed probability. Report actual shifted-law performance and keep any coverage/calibration claim separate.

The outer validation loss is ordinary expected regret, `tr(C(K(M_theta)) M_validation)`. The envelope derivative `gamma^2 (gamma I-C)^-1 - gamma I` instead describes the optimized robust value's sensitivity to its own nominal moment. Treating those as the same derivative would train a different objective. A separate robust-transfer bound uses both estimated and true optimal dual pairs; it is deterministic and is not a finite-sample generalization theorem.

## Falsifiable study claim

A suitable eventual result statement is: “Under the declared known-dynamics synthetic study, we tested whether choosing the same second-moment shrinkage family by validation control regret changes held-out policy quality relative to likelihood fitting. We verified the causal robust solver and implicit derivatives independently, and characterized an exact covariance-error ordering reversal and a spectral-margin transfer bound.” Add the measured positive or negative outcome and its interval after the preregistered experiment. Do not replace that evidence with a claim that combining established methods is inherently novel.
