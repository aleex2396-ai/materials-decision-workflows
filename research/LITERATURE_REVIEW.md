# Evidence for uncertainty-aware materials decisions and control

Research date: 2026-11-05. This is a targeted methods review, not an exhaustive survey or proof of novelty. Searches used public methods, paper titles and bibliographic identifiers.

## Decision question

How can a small-data materials workflow carry uncertainty from prediction through experiment selection and constrained control, and show exactly when its claims fail? The review informs a reproducible implementation with explicit assumptions, negative results and matched ablations.

## Retrieval and coverage

The public evidence bundle contains selected paper-level OpenAlex DOI/arXiv lookups with retrieval URLs and timestamps. Relevant primary manuscripts were checked separately. Broad exploratory retrievals and unrelated records are not part of the public bundle. `retrieve_openalex.ps1` repeats the listed methodological lookups without embedded credentials.

Some anonymous keyword and title searches received HTTP 429 responses, including the SafeOpt title query. Exact-identifier lookups supplied the paper records used in the public bibliography. OpenAlex is a discovery/provenance index, not evidence that a theorem was checked; primary pages and full papers below supplied the scientific claims. Searches also covered safe exploration, conformal inference under shift, materials Bayesian optimisation and public software. Coverage is thin on experimental materials benchmarks, nonlinear robust MPC and the newest 2026 papers; no novelty claim rests on this review.

## What this changes

Use one inspectable probabilistic surrogate and honest baselines before a neural architecture. Keep data provenance, unit/schema validation, grouped temporal splits and untouched evaluation separate from model fitting. Distinguish epistemic uncertainty, observation noise and misspecification. Report prediction error, uncertainty coverage/width, feasible discovery rate, optimisation regret/cost, and closed-loop constraint violations as different outcomes.

The recommended core is explicit bounded-uncertainty optimisation and receding-horizon control with a derivation that matches the executable model. If radius calibration uses residuals, separate training from calibration and state what distribution those residuals represent. A synthetic simulator is a methodological testbed; physical validity requires separate experimental evidence.

The main falsifiable study should ask whether better average prediction improves constrained decisions. Test the contrary possibility: a lower-RMSE model can make worse decisions because its error is concentrated near the constraint or optimum. Include nominal optimisation, uncertainty-aware optimisation, random acquisition, exploitation-only acquisition and a misspecified-uncertainty ablation. Keep negative findings.

## Claim boundaries that belong in the code/report

1. **Prediction versus selection:** marginal coverage over random inputs does not certify the input chosen by optimisation. A proof needs simultaneous coverage over the relevant candidates, a valid uncertainty set for a shared unknown parameter, or a selection-specific argument.
2. **Finite horizon versus persistent safety:** a feasible planned horizon does not establish recursive feasibility or stability. Specify a terminal set/backup policy and prove invariance if making those stronger claims; otherwise report empirical finite-horizon results.
3. **Set robustness versus distributional robustness:** worst-case over disturbances and worst-case over probability laws are different optimisation problems.
4. **Simulation versus experiment:** synthetic/public benchmarks substantiate algorithm behaviour. Private retrospective tests substantiate only their declared measured target. Neither establishes deployed process control without suitable dynamical measurements.
5. **Reproduction versus novelty:** GP regression, conformal calibration, Bayesian optimisation and MPC are established methods. A new repository is not a new method. Novelty remains unproven unless a precise new result survives comparison with relevant work.

## A useful counterexample to implement

Consider two input regions, A with probability 0.9 and B with probability 0.1. A predictor's interval always covers in A and never covers in B. It has exactly 90% marginal coverage. If optimistic objective estimates make the optimiser choose B on every run, selected-design coverage is zero. This elementary construction proves that the implication from marginal predictive coverage to selected-design safety is invalid; it does not prove that any particular implemented method fails. A stochastic version with repeated calibration datasets can quantify the failure and test a simultaneous-bound repair.

For a finite collection of M candidates, if each candidate has a valid error event probability at most alpha/M under the actual data-generation and evaluation setup, the union bound gives simultaneous error at most alpha. Dependence between candidates is allowed. However, ordinary marginal split conformal over heterogeneous X does not by itself supply those per-candidate guarantees. State the extra model or sampling assumption used to obtain them.

## Minimum research evidence

Pre-register budgets, seeds, split/group rules, candidate domain and hypotheses before using final test outcomes. Compare methods with paired random disturbances, repeated training/calibration samples, uncertainty intervals across independent repeats, and separate in-distribution, shifted and model-mismatch scenarios. Include computation time and infeasible-solver behaviour. A method that avoids every violation by taking no useful action must pay for that conservatism in the objective/abstention metric.

The implemented study focuses on task-dependent moment estimation, causal Wasserstein regret control, and the scale-versus-shape mechanism tested in the supplementary experiment. Claims follow the reported outcomes and the more specific [decision-focused review](DECISION_FOCUSED_REVIEW.md).
